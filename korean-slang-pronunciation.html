How Korean slang actually sounds

Slang is mostly short clipped words, and that makes the sound changes more obvious, not less. 국룰 isn't "guk-rul". People say 궁눌.

Most Korean slang is built by cutting a phrase down to its first syllables: 국룰 is 국민 룰, a rule everyone follows, and 중꺾마 is 중요한 건 꺾이지 않는 마음. Once the syllables are pushed together, the normal pronunciation rules kick in exactly as they would for any other word. So if you know the rules, you can read slang you've never seen. Below, each group is one rule, with the spelling on the left and what people say on the right.

If the rules themselves are new to you, start with Why Korean doesn't sound the way it's spelled and come back.

1. Nasalization: the biggest surprise

A ㄱ, ㄷ or ㅂ sound before ㄴ or ㅁ turns into ㅇ, ㄴ or ㅁ. In slang the ㄹ often becomes ㄴ first, and then the change before it follows.

WrittenSaidMeaning
국룰궁눌"a rule everyone follows", the obvious choice
중꺾마중껑마what matters is a heart that doesn't break
저속노화저송노화slow aging, as a lifestyle trend

2. Tense consonants: everything gets harder

After a ㄱ, ㄷ or ㅂ sound, the next plain consonant is said tense. Clipped words put those sounds side by side all the time.

WrittenSaidMeaning
갓생갇쌩a productive, well-planned life
덕질덕찔being a devoted fan of something
갑분싸갑뿐싸the mood suddenly died
맛도리맏또리really tasty
킹받다킹받따to be really annoyed
쩝쩝박사쩝쩝빡싸someone who knows every good food combo

3. Linking: the final consonant moves over

When the next syllable starts with a vowel, the consonant before it carries across.

WrittenSaidMeaning
얼죽아얼주가iced americano even if I freeze
밥약바뱍a plan to grab a meal together
오운완오우놘today's workout, done

4. Final sounds: only seven endings

A syllable can only end in seven sounds, so ㅅ, ㅆ, ㅈ, ㅊ, ㅌ and ㅎ all end up as a short ㄷ when nothing follows.

WrittenSaidMeaning
존맛존맏super delicious
느좋느졷nice vibe
인생샷인생샫the best photo of your life

5. ㅎ merges with its neighbor

ㅎ next to ㄱ, ㄷ or ㅂ combines with it into ㅋ, ㅌ or ㅍ. This one hides inside the clipped forms.

WrittenSaidMeaning
좋댓구알조탣꾸알like, comment, subscribe, notifications on
많관부만콴부"please show a lot of interest"

A note on using slang

Most of these are casual. 킹받다 or 존맛 with friends is fine; with a teacher, a boss or someone older you just met, it can come across badly. When in doubt, hear how people around you use a word before you use it yourself.
